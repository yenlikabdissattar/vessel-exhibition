    About

    Can a vessel hold a personality the way it holds water?

    A flower pot is a vessel. It holds something living, and it has a shape, a surface and a weight of its own. VESSEL asks which one you would be: not from a quiz, but from the words you choose or the colours you wear today.

    No two vessels are the same. Form, glaze, clay and flowers are all decided in the moment you arrive.

    The system rests on a colour study: a map of emotions drawn on two axes, pleasure and arousal, with a colour placed at each feeling. Every word you give, and every colour the camera sees, becomes a point on that map.

    At the exhibition, four Claude agents read each visitor: an Observer, a Vessel Maker, a Poet and a Director. This web version uses the same map and rules in your browser.

    1. A point on the map

      Words are placed by how pleasant and how energetic they feel; colours by where they sit in the study.

    2. Glaze and flowers

      The glaze is mixed from the nearest colours. Calm points get analogous flowers, restless ones a complementary clash.

    3. Form and surface

      Soft, settled feelings throw round jars; tense ones tall bottles; energy shows as fluting, dots or an unruly wobble.
